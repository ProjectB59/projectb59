B59-802.008 · WRITING · NEURAL NETS

Generative Adversarial Nets

I. J. Goodfellow et al. · arXiv:1406.2661 · 2014
“We propose a new framework for estimating generative models via an adversarial process, in which we simultaneously train two models: a generative model G … and a discriminative model D.”

Why it matters

GANs turned generative modelling into a game. A generator tries to produce samples that pass for real; a discriminator tries to tell real from fake. Training is the two networks competing, and at equilibrium the generator's distribution matches the data. No explicit likelihood is needed.

The effect

For most of the decade GANs produced the sharpest synthetic images available, and drove work on photorealistic faces, image-to-image translation and super-resolution. They also made "deepfake" a public concern.

Lineage

The adversarial idea spread into domain adaptation, representation learning and robustness. Diffusion models have since overtaken GANs for image synthesis, but the framing of training as a minimax game remains influential.

SOURCE: Ian J. Goodfellow et al., "Generative Adversarial Nets," arXiv:1406.2661 (2014); also in Advances in Neural Information Processing Systems 27 (2014).
