B59-802.001 · WRITING · NEURAL NETS

The Perceptron: A Probabilistic Model for Information Storage and Organization in the Brain

F. Rosenblatt · Psychological Review · 1958
“The perceptron is designed to illustrate some of the fundamental properties of intelligent systems in general, without becoming too deeply enmeshed in the special … conditions which hold for particular biological organisms.”

Why it matters

Rosenblatt turned the McCulloch and Pitts neuron into a machine that learns. The perceptron adjusts connection strengths in response to its errors, so that a system given examples can converge on a rule nobody programmed. He proved a convergence theorem: if a linear separation of the data exists, the learning procedure will find one in finite time.

What it got right and wrong

Right: weight updating from error is the seed of essentially all modern learning. Wrong, or at least oversold at the time: a single layer of these units can only separate linearly, a limit Minsky and Papert set out in detail eleven years later.

Lineage

The perceptron learning rule leads directly to the linear classifiers of today and, once stacked into layers and trained by backpropagation, to deep networks.

SOURCE: F. Rosenblatt, "The Perceptron: A Probabilistic Model for Information Storage and Organization in the Brain," Psychological Review, vol. 65, no. 6 (1958), pp. 386 to 408.
