B59-805.005 · WRITING · SAFETY & ALIGNMENT

Deep Reinforcement Learning from Human Preferences

P. Christiano et al. · arXiv:1706.03741 · 2017
“We explore goals defined in terms of (non-expert) human preferences between pairs of trajectory segments … This approach can effectively solve complex RL tasks without access to the reward function.”

Why it matters

Instead of hand-coding a reward function, which is where many safety failures start, the system learns one from humans comparing short clips of its behaviour and saying which they prefer. A reward model fitted to those comparisons then drives ordinary reinforcement learning. The paper showed this works on Atari and simulated robotics with feedback on well under one percent of the agent's interactions.

Why it is a safety paper

It is a practical method for aligning a system with human intent when that intent is hard to write down. It made learning from human feedback a real technique rather than a hope.

Lineage

This is the direct technical ancestor of RLHF, the method used to fine-tune InstructGPT, ChatGPT, Claude and the other assistant models that followed.

SOURCE: Paul Christiano, Jan Leike, Tom B. Brown, Miljan Martic, Shane Legg and Dario Amodei, "Deep Reinforcement Learning from Human Preferences," arXiv:1706.03741 (2017); also in Advances in Neural Information Processing Systems 30 (2017).
