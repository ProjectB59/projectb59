A Mathematical Theory of Communication

Claude E. Shannon · Bell System Technical Journal · 1948
“The fundamental problem of communication is that of reproducing at one point either exactly or approximately a message selected at another point.”

Why it matters

Shannon separated information from meaning and made it measurable. A message is a choice among possibilities; the information it carries is the number of yes or no questions needed to pin that choice down. The unit is the bit. From this he derived channel capacity, the role of redundancy, and the theorem that error-free communication is possible up to a hard limit set by noise.

The reach of it

Entropy, coding, compression and the capacity bound underlie every digital system built since. For machine learning the same quantity reappears everywhere: cross-entropy loss, information gain in decision trees, the description-length view of learning, mutual-information objectives.
