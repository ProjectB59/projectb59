Long Short-Term Memory

Sepp Hochreiter & Jürgen Schmidhuber · Neural Computation · 1997
“LSTM can learn to bridge time intervals in excess of 1000 steps even in case of noisy, incompressible input sequences, without loss of short time lag capabilities.”

Why it matters

Plain recurrent networks cannot hold information for long: gradients shrink or blow up as they travel back through time, a problem Hochreiter had diagnosed in 1991. LSTM fixes it with a memory cell whose contents pass forward unchanged unless explicit gates decide to write, read or erase. Error can flow across hundreds of steps without vanishing.

The reach of it

For roughly two decades LSTM was the default for sequence data: speech recognition, machine translation, handwriting, time series. It was in production at large scale in phones and translation services through the 2010s.

Lineage

The gating idea carries into GRUs, highway networks and the residual connections of very deep models. Transformers later displaced LSTM for many language tasks, but on long streaming sequences the gated-memory approach is still competitive.

SOURCE: Sepp Hochreiter and Jürgen Schmidhuber, "Long Short-Term Memory," Neural Computation, vol. 9, no. 8 (1997), pp. 1735 to 1780.
