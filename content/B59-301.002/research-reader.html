Cracking DES: Secrets of Encryption Research, Wiretap Politics, and Chip Design

The EFF’s 1998 book documenting "Deep Crack," the US $250,000 machine it built to break the Data Encryption Standard by brute force. The machine won RSA’s DES Challenge II in July 1998, recovering a key in about 56 hours and disproving government claims that cracking DES required multimillion-dollar resources and months of time. Because export controls barred publishing the design as software, the EFF published it as a printed book and placed it in the public domain. This transcription carries Whitfield Diffie’s foreword, the preface and Chapter 1 overview by John Gilmore, and the design and cryptanalysis chapters.

The EFF’s 1998 book documenting "Deep Crack," the US $250,000 machine it built to break the Data Encryption Standard by brute force. The machine won RSA’s DES Challenge II in July 1998, recovering a key in about 56 hours and disproving government claims that cracking DES required multimillion-dollar resources and months of time.
