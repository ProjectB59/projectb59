The Digital Path: Smart Contracts and the Third World

An essay by Mark S. Miller and Marc Stiegler responding to Hernando de Soto's The Mystery of Capital. Where de Soto argues that the developing world's informally held assets fail to become capital because national legal systems will not recognize them, Miller and Stiegler propose building the enforcement machinery of contract, title and rights transfer directly as capability-secure software, so that credible property-rights transfer "at a distance" can be bootstrapped without first reforming the state. An early and unusually concrete statement of the case for smart contracts as institutional infrastructure. Published in the collection Markets, Information and Communication (Routledge).

Mark S. Miller & Marc Stiegler · 2003-01-01 · writing

De Soto hopes to bring the power of capital to the poor by reforming the legal systems and bureaucracies of their national governments. This is no easy task ... We propose an alternative, complementary path: build the machinery of contract and property-rights transfer as software, on capability-secure distributed systems, so that it can spread without waiting for the state.
