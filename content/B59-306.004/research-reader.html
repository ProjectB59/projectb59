Privacy, Technology, and the Open Society

Speech delivered by John Gilmore at the First Conference on Computers, Freedom, and Privacy on March 28, 1991. Gilmore argues that the free and open society is being 'nibbled to death by ducks' through small, unheralded changes: the erosion of cash transactions, mandatory identification, mass incarceration, and the danger of census and phone records being used for persecution. He draws parallels to Nazi use of European phone records and Japanese-American internment via census data. Explicitly placed in the public domain by the author.
