B59-802.004 · WRITING · NEURAL NETS

Learning Internal Representations by Error Propagation

D. E. Rumelhart, G. E. Hinton & R. J. Williams · ICS Report 8506, UC San Diego · 1985
“The aim is … to find a learning procedure capable of building internal representations which are adequate for the task at hand.”

Why it matters

This is the technical-report version of the paper that made deep networks trainable. Backpropagation applies the chain rule to send error signals from the output back through the hidden layers, so every weight in a multilayer network gets a gradient telling it which way to move. The single-layer limit that had stalled the field since 1969 was gone.

Internal representations

The title is the point. Hidden units are not wired by hand; the learning procedure discovers what they should detect. Trained on a task, a network invents features. That is the core idea of representation learning.

This version

Issued as Institute for Cognitive Science Report 8506 (September 1985) and expanded as Chapter 8 of the Parallel Distributed Processing volumes (1986); the compressed Nature letter appeared the same year. The technical report is the fuller account.

SOURCE: David E. Rumelhart, Geoffrey E. Hinton and Ronald J. Williams, "Learning Internal Representations by Error Propagation," ICS Report 8506, Institute for Cognitive Science, University of California San Diego, September 1985.
