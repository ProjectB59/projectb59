Learning to Predict by the Methods of Temporal Differences

Richard S. Sutton · Machine Learning · 1988
“Whereas conventional prediction-learning methods assign credit by means of the difference between predicted and actual outcomes, the new methods assign credit by means of the difference between temporally successive predictions.”

Why it matters

Temporal-difference learning lets a system learn to predict long-run outcomes without waiting for them. It updates each prediction toward the next prediction, so learning happens step by step during an experience rather than only at the end. Sutton proved convergence for the linear case and showed TD can be both faster and more data-efficient than supervised alternatives.

The core of RL

TD is the idea that makes reinforcement learning practical. Bootstrapping, learning a guess from a guess, is what separates RL's value methods from plain Monte Carlo estimation.

Lineage

TD-Gammon, Q-learning, actor-critic methods, and the deep RL systems that play Atari and Go all rest on this. TD signals also turned out to match dopamine activity in the brain, tying the algorithm to neuroscience.

SOURCE: Richard S. Sutton, "Learning to Predict by the Methods of Temporal Differences," Machine Learning, vol. 3 (1988), pp. 9 to 44.
