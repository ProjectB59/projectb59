Satoshi Uesaka: Game Arts and the Thexder Line

Project B59 Research · assembled 1 September 2026 · profile record, subclass 9.04 (game & arcade history)
A working credit history, not a biography: the Japanese-PC-gaming figure who did graphic and mechanical design on Thexder and Silpheed, then directed the Thexder sequel, told through his canonical dated credit list.

Why this is in the vault

Class 9 of the collection covers virtual worlds and games; subclass 9.04 covers game and arcade history, the record of how the medium and its craft actually developed. Most of that subclass so far is North American: Spacewar!, Colossal Cave, the coin-op arcade, APh Technological Consulting and Intellivision. Satoshi Uesaka (上坂 哲) is a small, concrete entry from the other major branch: the Japanese home-computer scene of the mid-1980s, built on NEC's PC-8801 and its contemporaries rather than on consoles or arcade boards.

Uesaka worked at Game Arts, the Tokyo studio founded in 1985 by Yoichi and Takeshi Miyaji. Game Arts made its name with technically aggressive action games for general-purpose personal computers, and Uesaka is a representative craftsman of that period: someone who moved fluidly across graphic design, mechanical and level design, programming, and direction, on titles that were among the most demonstrated pieces of software on the PC-88. He is not a cypherpunk, a cryptographer, or an economist, and there is no claim here that he is. He is a data point in the history of the personal computer as a place where games were built by hand, which is the reason class 9 exists.

What he actually did

Thexder (Game Arts, NEC PC-8801, April 1985) is the anchor. It is a run-and-gun game about a robot that transforms into a jet, and it was a commercial breakout for the young studio, later licensed to Square for a Famicom conversion and ported across more than a dozen platforms. Wikipedia's infobox credits its design to Hibiki Godai and Satoshi Uesaka; secondary Japanese sources describe Uesaka's contribution as graphic and mechanical design, meaning the sprite art and the moving parts of how the game plays.

Silpheed (Game Arts, PC-8801, December 1986) is the follow-up landmark: a forced-scrolling shooter rendered with filled-polygon 3D, unusual on that hardware. It was directed by Takeshi Miyaji; Uesaka is credited as one of its programmers and one of its artists. On Fire Hawk: Thexder – The Second Contact (テグザー2 ファイアーホーク, Game Arts, MSX2 / MS-DOS / PC-8801, 1989), the direct sequel to Thexder, Uesaka moves up: he is credited as a director (with Ari Kamijō and Kazuyuki Ohata) and as the game's writer. The task brief that prompted this record describes him as "director and lead graphic designer" on Fire Hawk; the credits that can be verified here say director and writer, with dedicated design credit going to Kamijō and Osamu Harada. That discrepancy is left visible rather than smoothed over.

After the 1980s his credits shift toward production and management, mostly on Game Arts' Sega CD and Saturn output (the Lunar and Grandia years) and later on Project Sylpheed (2006) and Thexder Neo (2009). MobyGames lists him under several romanizations and handles: S. Uesaka, Tetsu Uesaka, and the recurring nickname "Gappa."

Sourcing and what is unverified
