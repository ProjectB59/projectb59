B59-400.002 · MAILING-LIST THREAD · SATOSHI · SCALING

Satoshi Nakamoto ↔ James A. Donald

"Bitcoin P2P e-cash paper" · cryptography@metzdowd.com · 31 October – 17 November 2008
“We very, very much need such a system, but the way I understand your proposal, it does not seem to scale to the required size.”

When Satoshi Nakamoto announced the Bitcoin white paper to the cryptography mailing list on 31 October 2008, the first substantive reply came from James A. Donald, writing as jamesd@echeque.com — a long-time cypherpunk and author of the Crypto Kong signature tool (see B59-203.005, B59-105.004).

Donald pressed the scaling question harder than anyone else in the thread: for proof-of-work tokens to hold value they must circulate on a very large network, but timely double-spend detection seems to require every peer to hold most of the transaction history, and "if hundreds of millions of people are doing transactions, that is a lot of bandwidth." Satoshi’s replies in this thread contain some of his clearest early explanations of block propagation, SPV-style verification, and the incentive argument.

The complete October and November 2008 list archives are attached as the primary source. The excerpt below is Donald’s opening reply of 3 November 2008.

James A. Donald, 3 November 2008

From jamesd at echeque.com Sun Nov 2 18:46:23 2008 From: jamesd at echeque.com (James A. Donald) Date: Mon, 03 Nov 2008 09:46:23 +1000 Subject: Bitcoin P2P e-cash paper In-Reply-To: <CHILKAT-MID-dd013ecc-8c7f-fc14-b1c5-cc9fd0181098@server123> References: <CHILKAT-MID-dd013ecc-8c7f-fc14-b1c5-cc9fd0181098@server123> Message-ID: <490E3BCF.2020109@echeque.com> Satoshi Nakamoto wrote: > I've been working on a new electronic cash system that's fully > peer-to-peer, with no trusted third party. > > The paper is available at: > http://www.bitcoin.org/bitcoin.pdf We very, very much need such a system, but the way I understand your proposal, it does not seem to scale to the required size. For transferable proof of work tokens to have value, they must have monetary value. To have monetary value, they must be transferred within a very large network - for example a file trading network akin to bittorrent. To detect and reject a double spending event in a timely manner, one must have most past transactions of the coins in the transaction, which, naively implemented, requires each peer to have most past transactions, or most past transactions that occurred recently. If hundreds of millions of people are doing transactions, that is a lot of bandwidth - each must know all, or a substantial part thereof. ---------------------------------------------------------------------
