B59-802.003 · WRITING · NEURAL NETS

Neural Networks and Physical Systems with Emergent Collective Computational Abilities

J. J. Hopfield · PNAS · 1982
“Computational properties of use to biological organisms … can emerge as collective properties of systems having a large number of simple equivalent components.”

Why it matters

Hopfield, a physicist, showed that a network of simple symmetric units settles into stable states that can be read as stored memories. Recall becomes a matter of the system rolling downhill to the nearest low point of an energy function. Feed in a corrupted or partial pattern and the network relaxes to the complete stored one: content-addressable memory as physics.

The bridge

Casting the network as an energy landscape connected neural computation to statistical mechanics and spin glasses, and brought a generation of physicists into the field. It made recurrent networks analysable.

Lineage

The energy view runs forward to Boltzmann machines, to modern "modern Hopfield networks" and their link to attention, and it was recognised by the 2024 Nobel Prize in Physics.

SOURCE: J. J. Hopfield, "Neural Networks and Physical Systems with Emergent Collective Computational Abilities," Proceedings of the National Academy of Sciences USA, vol. 79, no. 8 (April 1982), pp. 2554 to 2558.
