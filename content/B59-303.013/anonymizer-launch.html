The Anonymizer

Lance Cottrell / Anonymizer, Inc. · 1997-04-03
Because on today's Internet, people do know you're a dog.

The Anonymizer took the mix idea and pointed it at the web: instead of your browser talking to a site, it talked to Cottrell's proxy, which talked to the site, so the site saw the proxy and not you. It was one of the first privacy tools aimed at ordinary users rather than cypherpunks, and it stayed in operation, under Cottrell, far longer than most of its contemporaries.

The literal 1995 launch notice could not be found in the archives available here; this 1997 capture is the earliest primary artifact of the service and is hosted in its place. See the manifest.

ANONYMOUS SURFING "Because on today's Internet, people do know you're a dog" Many people surf the web under the illusion that their actions are private and anonymous. Unfortunately, it isn't so. Every time you visit a site, you leave a calling card that reveals where you're coming from, what kind of computer you have, and other details. Most sites keep logs of all your visits. In many cases, this logging may constitute a violation of your privacy. Our "anonymizer" service allows you to surf the web without revealing any personal information. It is fast, it is easy, and it is free.
