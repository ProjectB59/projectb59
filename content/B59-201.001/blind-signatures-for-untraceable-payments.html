B59-201.001 · WRITING · BLIND SIGNATURES

Blind Signatures for Untraceable Payments

David Chaum · Advances in Cryptology, Proceedings of CRYPTO ’82 · Santa Barbara, August 1982
A signature you can obtain on a message without the signer seeing the message, the cryptographic core of anonymous digital cash.

Why it matters

Chaum’s mix networks (B59-303.001) anonymized communication. This paper anonymizes money. It is the first construction that lets a bank issue currency it cannot trace, and it defined the problem, payer anonymity with issuer control and double-spend detection, that digital cash has worked on ever since.

The mechanism

The user multiplies the coin value by a random blinding factor before sending it to the bank. The bank signs the blinded value (debiting the account) and returns it. The user divides out the blinding factor, leaving a genuine bank signature on the original coin. The bank has never seen that coin and cannot recognize it at deposit, but the signature is valid and unforgeable.

The lineage it started

Chaum founded DigiCash in 1989 to commercialize this; its ecash system ran trials with several banks before the company failed in 1998 (see B59-001.005). The idea outlived the company: Chaumian ecash is being actively rebuilt today in Bitcoin mints such as Cashu and Fedimint.
