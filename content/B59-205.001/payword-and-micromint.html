B59-205.001 · WRITING · MICROPAYMENTS

PayWord and MicroMint: Two Simple Micropayment Schemes

Ronald L. Rivest and Adi Shamir · Security Protocols Workshop, Cambridge · April 1996
"Our main goal is to minimize the number of public-key operations required per payment, using hash operations instead whenever possible."

Why it matters

The micropayment problem, how to charge a fraction of a cent without spending more than that in processing, drove a decade of pre-Bitcoin digital-cash design. PayWord’s answer, amortize one signature over a chain of cheap hashes, is the same trick that reappears in probabilistic payments (B59-205.002) and later in Lightning-style payment channels.

The two schemes

PayWord: the user commits to the end of a hash chain with a single signed message, then releases pre-images one at a time as payment; the vendor redeems only the last one it received. MicroMint: coins are k-way hash collisions, whose cost per coin drops sharply at scale, so only a large broker can mint them economically.

The lineage it started

Hash chains as payment appear again in Rivest’s lottery-ticket scheme, in NetBill and Millicent-era systems, and structurally in the HTLC and channel-update mechanics of the Lightning Network.

From the introduction

We present two simple micropayment schemes, "PayWord" and "MicroMint," for making small purchases over the Internet. ... Our main goal is to minimize the number of public-key operations required per payment, using hash operations instead whenever possible. As a rough guide, hash functions are about 100 times faster than RSA signature verification, and about 10,000 times faster than RSA signature generation.
