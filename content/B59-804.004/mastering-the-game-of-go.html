Mastering the Game of Go with Deep Neural Networks and Tree Search

D. Silver et al. (DeepMind) · Nature · 2016
“Using this search algorithm, our program AlphaGo achieved a 99.8% winning rate against other Go programs, and defeated the human European Go champion by 5 games to 0.”

Why it matters

Go had been the standing benchmark for what machines could not do: too many positions to search, too much reliance on pattern and judgement. AlphaGo combined a policy network that proposes moves and a value network that evaluates positions with Monte Carlo tree search. Months after this paper it beat Lee Sedol, one of the strongest players alive, 4 to 1.

How it learned

Supervised training on human games to start, then reinforcement learning from millions of games of self-play. The later AlphaGo Zero dropped the human games entirely.

Lineage

AlphaGo Zero, then AlphaZero (chess, shogi, Go from self-play alone), then MuZero (no rules given). The self-play recipe now reaches well beyond board games.

SOURCE: David Silver et al., "Mastering the Game of Go with Deep Neural Networks and Tree Search," Nature, vol. 529 (28 January 2016), pp. 484 to 489.
