B59-101.006 · WRITING · PUBLIC-KEY CRYPTOGRAPHY

The First Ten Years of Public-Key Cryptography

Whitfield Diffie · Proceedings of the IEEE, 76(5) · May 1988
Diffie’s own history of the idea he and Hellman set loose, written from inside it, a decade on.

Why it matters

This is the account of public key cryptography’s first decade written by the person best placed to write it. It is the source most later histories lean on for the sequence of events between 1974 and 1984, and for Diffie’s own framing of what the invention did and did not settle.

What it argues

Diffie separates the concept (splitting the ability to encrypt from the ability to decrypt) from the specific systems that implement it, and shows how fragile the early systems were: several knapsack proposals had already fallen by 1988. He treats digital signatures and protocol design as the parts of the problem still wide open, which is roughly where the cypherpunks picked the thread up.

On credit

The paper is careful about attribution, giving Merkle’s puzzle work its due as an independent origin and discussing the claims that would later be confirmed about parallel classified work at GCHQ. It set the tone for how the field talks about its own history.
