Rubberhose (Marutukku) — Project Page

Julian Assange, Ralf-P. Weinmann, Suelette Dreyfus · 2000-10-18
Rubberhose transparently and deniably encrypts disk data, minimising the effectiveness of warrants, coercive interrogations and other compulsive mechanisms, such as U.K. RIP legislation.

Rubberhose is Assange's cryptographic engineering, as distinct from his writing. The threat it targets is the one the name states: not an attacker who breaks the cipher but one who beats the passphrase out of you. Its answer is deniability, a disk that can always yield "a" key without revealing whether it has yielded "the" key, so that no amount of coercion can establish that anything is still hidden.

The work fed directly into the essays. The problem of what compulsion can and cannot extract from a person is the same problem the conspiracy essays approach from the side of institutions.

Full text

Rubberhose cryptographically deniable transparent disk encryption system Rubberhose transparently and deniably encrypts disk data, minimising the effectiveness of warrants, coersive interrogations and other compulsive mechanims, such as U.K RIP legislation. Rubberhose differs from conventional disk encryption systems in that it has an advanced modular architecture, self-test suite, is more secure, portable, utilises information hiding (steganography / deniable cryptography), works with any file system and has source freely available. Currently supported ciphers are DES, 3DES, IDEA, RC5, RC6, Blowfish, Twofish and CAST. Written by Julian Assange, Ralf P. Weinmann and Suelette Dreyfus, Rubberhose is currently available for Linux 2.2. Userland daemons and tools are highly portable. NetBSD & FreeBSD kernel modules are nearing completion.
