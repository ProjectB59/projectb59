B59-802.006 · WRITING · NEURAL NETS

Gradient-Based Learning Applied to Document Recognition

Y. LeCun, L. Bottou, Y. Bengio & P. Haffner · Proceedings of the IEEE · 1998
“Better pattern recognition systems can be built by relying more on automatic learning and less on hand-designed heuristics.”

Why it matters

This long paper is the mature statement of the convolutional neural network. Local receptive fields, shared weights and spatial pooling let a network exploit the structure of images: features are detected wherever they occur, with far fewer parameters than a fully connected net. The LeNet-5 model here read handwritten digits well enough to run on a large share of United States bank cheques.

More than convnets

The second half introduces graph transformer networks: train an entire document-reading pipeline, segmentation included, end to end by gradient descent. The principle of optimising the whole system rather than hand-tuned stages is now standard.

Lineage

LeNet is the direct ancestor of AlexNet, and of essentially every image model that followed.

SOURCE: Y. LeCun, L. Bottou, Y. Bengio and P. Haffner, "Gradient-Based Learning Applied to Document Recognition," Proceedings of the IEEE, vol. 86, no. 11 (November 1998), pp. 2278 to 2324.
