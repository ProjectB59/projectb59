B59-101.004 · WRITING · PUBLIC-KEY CRYPTOGRAPHY

An Overview of Public Key Cryptography

Martin E. Hellman · IEEE Communications Society Magazine, 16(6) · November 1978
"With a public key cryptosystem, the key used to encipher a message can be made public without compromising the secrecy of a different key needed to decipher that message."

Why it matters

"New Directions" was a research paper in an information-theory journal. This is the version Hellman wrote to explain the same ideas to working engineers, in a communications trade magazine, at the exact moment public key cryptography was moving from a small circle into industry. It is a primary record of how the field first pitched itself.

What it covers

Hellman walks through the commercial case for encryption (wiretapping, automated traffic analysis, electronic mail), then the two capabilities public key methods add: distributing keys over an open channel, and signing messages so a recipient can prove authorship to a third party. He compares the exponential key exchange, the trapdoor knapsack he and Merkle proposed, and the newly published RSA.

Reading it now

The knapsack system Hellman presents here as a leading candidate was broken by Shamir in 1982. That does not date the article so much as show the period honestly: several trapdoor constructions were in play, and it was not yet obvious which would survive.

From the opening

Cryptography has been of great importance to the military and diplomatic communities since antiquity but failed, until recently, to attract much commercial attention. Recent commercial interest, by contrast, has been almost explosive due to the rapid computerization of information storage, transmission, and spying.
SOURCE: Martin E. Hellman, "An Overview of Public Key Cryptography," IEEE Communications Society Magazine, vol. 16, no. 6, November 1978, pp. 24-32. (Reprinted in IEEE Communications Magazine, 50th Anniversary issue, May 2002.)
