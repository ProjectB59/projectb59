B59-402.004 · WRITING · PROOF-OF-WORK

On Memory-Bound Functions for Fighting Spam

Cynthia Dwork, Andrew Goldberg and Moni Naor · Advances in Cryptology — CRYPTO 2003 · August 2003
"Memory access speeds vary across machines much less than do CPU speeds, [so] memory-bound functions may behave more equitably than CPU-bound functions."

Why it matters

It is the missing middle of the proof-of-work story. Between the 1992 origin (B59-402.001) and Bitcoin’s 2008 hash-based mining, researchers already saw the central weakness of CPU puzzles, hardware asymmetry, and tried to fix it. The "memory-hard" and "ASIC-resistant" functions later used by Litecoin (scrypt), Ethereum (Ethash) and others are pursuing exactly the goal set here.

What it proves

A formal model of a memory-bound computation, an abstract function with an asymptotically tight amortized lower bound on required memory accesses for the sender, a concrete instantiation inspired by RC4, a way for the receiver to verify with no memory accesses, and measurements showing only a 4x spread between a set-top box and a fast workstation.

The lineage it started

The memory-hardness research line runs from here through Percival’s scrypt (2009) to Argon2 and the egalitarian-mining designs, and informs every later argument about whether proof-of-work can stay accessible to ordinary hardware.

Original abstract (excerpt)

In 1992, Dwork and Naor proposed that e-mail messages be accompanied by easy-to-check proofs of computational effort in order to discourage junk e-mail, now known as spam. They proposed specific CPU-bound functions for this purpose. Burrows suggested that, since memory access speeds vary across machines much less than do CPU speeds, memory-bound functions may behave more equitably than CPU-bound functions... we prove that, on average, the sender of a message must perform many unrelated accesses to memory, while the receiver, in order to verify the work, has to perform significantly fewer accesses.
SOURCE: Cynthia Dwork, Andrew Goldberg, and Moni Naor, "On Memory-Bound Functions for Fighting Spam," in D. Boneh (ed.), Advances in Cryptology — CRYPTO 2003, Lecture Notes in Computer Science 2729, Springer, 2003, pp. 426-444.
