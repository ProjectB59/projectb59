A Digital Signature Based on a Conventional Encryption Function

Ralph C. Merkle's CRYPTO '87 paper introduces a digital-signature construction built from conventional one-way or symmetric cryptographic functions and authenticated through a tree of one-time public keys. The work is an important ancestor of modern hash-based signature systems and a key reference point for Merkle-tree authentication structures.

Ralph C. Merkle · CRYPTO '87 · Lecture Notes in Computer Science 293 · pp. 369–378

Historical significance

The paper develops a signature system whose security rests on conventional cryptographic functions rather than the number-theoretic assumptions used by RSA-style signatures. It combines one-time signatures with a tree structure that authenticates many public keys under a compact root.

This tree-authentication idea became foundational far beyond the original signature proposal. Merkle trees are now standard tools for efficiently proving membership and integrity in large data sets, and they later became a central component of Bitcoin's block structure.
