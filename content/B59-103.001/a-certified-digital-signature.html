B59-103.001 · WRITING · HASH TREES

A Certified Digital Signature

Ralph C. Merkle · written 1979, published in Advances in Cryptology — CRYPTO ’89
"A digital signature based only on a conventional encryption function... as secure as the underlying encryption function."

Why it matters

This paper shows you can build a general-purpose digital signature from a hash function alone, with no number theory, no factoring, no discrete logs. That makes the scheme immune to the quantum attacks that threaten RSA and elliptic-curve signatures, which is why hash-based signatures are back in the NIST post-quantum standards (SPHINCS+, XMSS, LMS) that trace directly to this construction.

What it builds

A one-time signature (Lamport style) lets you sign a single message by revealing pre-images of a hash. Merkle’s tree turns many one-time keys into one reusable public key: the root hash certifies every leaf, and each signature carries the authentication path proving its leaf belongs. The result signs an effectively unlimited number of messages, with signature size growing only logarithmically.

A note on this record

See the provenance field: this is the author-hosted "Certified Digital Signature" standing in for the paywalled 1987 CRYPTO paper of a similar name. The two papers develop the same family of ideas.

From the author’s 2012 note (in the hosted file)

This paper was originally submitted to Ron Rivest, then editor at the Communications of the ACM, in 1979. It was accepted subject to revisions, and was revised and resubmitted in November of 1979. Unfortunately, Ron Rivest passed over the editorship to someone else, the author became involved in a startup, and the referees reportedly never responded to the revised draft... It was finally published in Crypto 89, page 218.
