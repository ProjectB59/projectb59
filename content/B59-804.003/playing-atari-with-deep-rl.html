Playing Atari with Deep Reinforcement Learning

V. Mnih et al. (DeepMind) · arXiv:1312.5602 · 2013
“The model is a convolutional neural network, trained with a variant of Q-learning, whose input is raw pixels and whose output is a value function estimating future rewards.”

Why it matters

One network, one set of hyperparameters, learned to play seven Atari games from the screen pixels and the score alone, beating a human expert on three. It was the first convincing demonstration that deep networks and reinforcement learning could be combined to learn control policies straight from high-dimensional sensory input.

What made it stable

Two tricks that became standard: an experience replay buffer that breaks the correlation between consecutive training samples, and periodic freezing of the target network. Together they tamed the instability that had defeated earlier attempts to use neural nets as value functions.

Lineage

The 2015 Nature paper extended this to 49 games at human level. It launched deep reinforcement learning as a field and led directly to AlphaGo.

SOURCE: Volodymyr Mnih et al., "Playing Atari with Deep Reinforcement Learning," arXiv:1312.5602 (2013); NIPS 2013 Deep Learning Workshop.
