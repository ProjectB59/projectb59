B59-401.002 · PAPER · TIMESTAMPING · MERKLE TREE

Improving the Efficiency and Reliability of Digital Time-Stamping

Dave Bayer, Stuart Haber & W. Scott Stornetta · 1993
Combine an interval’s documents into a hash tree; one root value witnesses them all, and each proof is just the path to the root.

Two years after "How to Time-Stamp a Digital Document," the same authors — now with mathematician Dave Bayer — make the scheme practical. Instead of a single linked list of certificates, the documents submitted in each time period are combined with a Merkle hash tree.

That gives two things Bitcoin uses directly: one compact "witness" value (the tree root) fixes every document in the interval at once, and any individual document’s timestamp proof shrinks to the O(log n) sibling hashes on its path to the root. The paper also suggests publishing each period’s root value somewhere public and hard to alter — the authors’ later commercial service famously printed it in a newspaper.

A Bitcoin block is this construction: transactions committed by a Merkle root in the header, the header chained to its predecessor and witnessed by the whole network’s proof-of-work. The full PDF, from co-author Dave Bayer’s Columbia page, is attached.
