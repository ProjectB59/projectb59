Markets and Computation: Agoric Open Systems

The foundational "agoric systems" paper by Mark S. Miller and K. Eric Drexler, from The Ecology of Computation (North-Holland, 1988). It proposes organizing computation as a market: software objects that own resources, price them, and trade, with property rights enforced by an underlying capability security model, so that global allocation emerges from local decisions. This is the direct intellectual root of Miller's later object-capability work (E, Robust Composition, B59-505.004), of the Agoric company, and of the "high tech Hayekian" milieu documented in B59-505.002. This paper examines markets as a model for computation and proposes a framework - agoric systems - for applying the power of market mechanisms to the software domain. ... such a system should give rise to increasingly intelligent behavior as an emergent property of interactions among software entities and people.
