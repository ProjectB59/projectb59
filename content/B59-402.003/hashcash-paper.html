Hashcash — A Denial of Service Counter-Measure

Adam Back · technical report · 1 August 2002 (“5 years on”)
A parameterisable, publicly auditable cost-function: cheap to verify, tunably expensive to compute.

Written five years after the 1997 code, this is the reference description of hashcash. Back formalises the notion of a "cost-function" — a computation that is moderately hard to perform but cheap to check — and classifies such functions as interactive or non-interactive, publicly auditable or privately verifiable, and fixed-cost or probabilistic-cost.

The hashcash function itself is a partial pre-image (partial hash collision) problem: find a stamp whose hash has k leading zero bits. The paper gives the stamp format, discusses efficiency, pre-computation and the effect of Moore’s law, and reviews uses including DoS mitigation, Hal Finney’s reusable-proof-of-work tokens, and mail postage.

The full 10-page PDF is attached as the primary source. Its argument — unforgeable, tunable, publicly verifiable cost as a Sybil / flooding counter-measure — is the mechanism Bitcoin adopts for block production.
