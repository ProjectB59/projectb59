Q-learning

Christopher J. C. H. Watkins & Peter Dayan · Machine Learning · 1992
“Q-learning … provides agents with the capability of learning to act optimally in Markovian domains by experiencing the consequences of actions, without requiring them to build maps of the domains.”

Why it matters

Q-learning learns the value of taking an action in a state and then acting optimally afterward, directly from experience, with no model of the environment. Watkins and Dayan's contribution here is the convergence proof: under mild conditions the learned values converge to the optimal ones with probability one.

Why it stuck

It is off-policy: the agent can explore however it likes and still converge on the optimal policy. It is simple to implement. That combination made it the standard reinforcement-learning algorithm for decades.

Lineage

Deep Q-Networks (Atari, 2013 to 2015) are Q-learning with a neural network as the value function. The algorithm is still a baseline in current RL.

SOURCE: Christopher J. C. H. Watkins and Peter Dayan, "Q-learning," Machine Learning, vol. 8 (1992), pp. 279 to 292.
