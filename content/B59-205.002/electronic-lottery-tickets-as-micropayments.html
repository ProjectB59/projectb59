Electronic Lottery Tickets as Micropayments

Ronald L. Rivest · Financial Cryptography ’97, Anguilla · February 1997
"This scheme is exceptionally efficient since the bank handles only winning tickets, instead of handling each micropayment."

Why it matters

It reframes efficiency: you cannot make processing a one-cent payment cost less than a cent, but you can make most one-cent payments generate no processing at all. Expected value stays honest; variance does the work. This is the conceptual seed of "don’t settle every payment on the ledger."

The mechanism

A payment is a signed ticket that is valid only if some later-revealed value hashes into a winning range. The vendor accepts many losing tickets knowing the winners, scaled up in value, average out to what it is owed. Only winners are ever sent to the bank.

The lineage it started

Probabilistic and aggregated payments run through MICROPAYMENTS research into Peppercoin (Rivest and Micali), and the "bank sees only net results" principle is exactly what payment channels and rollups generalize.

Original abstract

We present a new micropayment scheme based on the use of "electronic lottery tickets." This scheme is exceptionally efficient since the bank handles only winning tickets, instead of handling each micropayment.
