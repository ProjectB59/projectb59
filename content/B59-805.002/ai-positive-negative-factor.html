B59-805.002 · WRITING · SAFETY & ALIGNMENT

Artificial Intelligence as a Positive and Negative Factor in Global Risk

Eliezer Yudkowsky · in Global Catastrophic Risks (OUP) · 2008
“The AI does not hate you, nor does it love you, but you are made out of atoms which it can use for something else.”

Why it matters

Yudkowsky's chapter is the long-form case that a sufficiently capable AI with goals not carefully aligned to human values is a global catastrophic risk, and that alignment is a hard technical problem rather than a matter of good intentions. It argues against anthropomorphism: a mind need not share human motivations, and "most" possible goals, pursued competently, are bad for us.

Key ideas

Intelligence as optimisation power aimed at a target; the difficulty of specifying a target that stays safe under great capability; the observation that a system able to improve itself could gain capability quickly; and "Friendly AI" as the name for getting the target right.

Lineage

This chapter and Bostrom's work are the two texts that moved AI risk from science fiction into a research programme, MIRI, later the safety teams at the major labs.

SOURCE: Eliezer Yudkowsky, "Artificial Intelligence as a Positive and Negative Factor in Global Risk," in Nick Bostrom and Milan M. Cirkovic, eds., Global Catastrophic Risks (Oxford University Press, 2008), pp. 308 to 345.
