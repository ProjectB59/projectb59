How to Time-Stamp a Digital Document

Stuart Haber & W. Scott Stornetta · Bellcore · Journal of Cryptology, 1991
Hash each new record together with the one before it, and the sequence becomes something no one can quietly rewrite.

This is where the block chain’s core trick comes from. Haber and Stornetta pose the problem of proving when a digital document existed, without having to trust whoever runs the timestamping service.

Their first mechanism is linking: the timestamp certificate for each document includes the hash of the previous certificate, so the certificates form a chain — altering one record means redoing every record after it. Their second is distributed trust: the document’s own hash pseudorandomly selects several independent parties who must each co-sign the timestamp, so no single party can backdate or forge.

The Bitcoin white paper cites this work; Satoshi’s "The network timestamps transactions by hashing them into an ongoing chain of hash-based proof-of-work" is this paper’s linking scheme with proof-of-work added. The full PDF is attached.
